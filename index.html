About Me

I study how baryons shape galaxy evolution and bias cosmological measurements, using cosmological simulations and new probes such as fast radio bursts.

I am a final-year Ph.D. candidate in the Astronomy Department at Yale University (expected May 2027), advised by Prof. Daisuke Nagai. I am currently applying for postdoctoral positions. For a full overview of my work, please see my Curriculum Vitae.

Research

The central question guiding my work is: what is the three-dimensional distribution and thermodynamic state of the baryons in our Universe? This is key for both galaxy formation and evolution, which is regulated by processes such as baryonic feedback and non-thermal pressure, and for cosmology, where baryonic effects are a leading source of uncertainty for Roman, Euclid, and LSST. To address this question, I use hydrodynamical cosmological simulations in combination with analytic models, focusing on two topics: fast radio bursts as cosmological probes and multi-scale filaments.

For more details on my work on fast radio bursts and multi-scale filaments, see my research page.

Outreach and Teaching

Leitner Planetarium and Observatory Spanish Language Public Nights

I am the creator and organizer of Spanish Language Public Nights at Leitner Planetarium and Observatory in New Haven, Connecticut. Once a semester, the planetarium hosts a night open to the public including planetarium shows in Spanish and night sky telescope viewing. I am currently working on expanding this event to happen three times a semester and develop more materials that can also be used at other facilities for similar events.

Yale Pathways

In partnership with the Yale Pathways program, I co-designed and led two introductory astrophysics workshops for New Haven public high school students. The first, in July 2025, was a two-day workshop on black holes and dark matter. In July 2026 we expanded it into a one-week program covering scales in astrophysics from the solar system to the cosmic web, with a focus on computational astrophysics through hands-on demos and a session in StarLab, a mobile planetarium. One of the major goals of our workshops was to show students what it looks like to do astrophysics research, and help them envision themselves as future researchers.

Yale SACNAS

I served as the co-president, secretary, and treasurer of the Yale chapter of the Society for Advancement of Chicanos/Hispanics and Native Americans in Science (SACNAS). As part of my responsibilities I helped lead and organize meetings, managed the budget, and organized outreach events. I was also part of the organizing committee for the 2025 SACNAS New England Community gathering our chapter led and hosted at Yale.

Public Talks

I have given talks targeted towards the local New Haven community through several avenues, including at local libraries through the Yale Science in the News program, for school visits at the Leitner Planetarium, and at the after school EVOLUTIONS program of the Peabody Museum.

Teaching with EduExplora

I designed and taught a two-week introductory course on Astronomy to Latin American middle schoolers and high schoolers visiting Yale University through the EduExplora summer program. As part of the course, students participated in several hands-on activities, including designing, building, and launching their own stomp rockets as well as creating and visualizing computationally their own solar systems.

Background

I am an Ecuadorian-American, born and raised in Philadelphia, where I attended a Spanish immersion elementary and middle school and a project-based high school. I graduated with honors from Princeton University in 2021 with an A.B. in Astrophysical Sciences and certificates in Applications of Computing and Russian Language, and joined the Yale Astronomy PhD program that fall. Outside of research, I am passionate about increasing the accessibility of astronomy in the Latinx community.
